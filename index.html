01 · What we were given

Understand the brief
before solving it.

The assignment asks for four connected business answers: define customer value, identify future high-value users early, make smarter channel decisions and reduce revenue concentration risk.

Assignment questions
QUESTION 01Customer value

Define high-value customers and show how they differ. Extract actionable insights.

QUESTION 02Weekly watchlist

Select 20 new users after their first 7 days who are likely to be high-value by day 90.

QUESTION 03Channel allocation

Recommend how marketing budget should be allocated across acquisition channels.

QUESTION 04Revenue resilience

Analyse revenue concentration and identify the next bet if a major source declines.

Data supplied

Common join key: third_party_id. Signup records span April–September 2026; order data runs through 31 October 2026.

02 · Overall approach

One analytical workflow.
Four business answers.

Build a consistent revenue base, define populations and time windows, analyse the patterns, validate the result and translate the evidence into an action.

STEP 01Prepare & validate

Inspect six files, join on third_party_id, standardise timestamps, statuses and product revenue.

STEP 02Define cohorts

Separate full-period Q1 positive-revenue users from mature 90-day prediction cohorts.

STEP 03Analyse & model

Segment revenue, engineer day-7 features, compare models and evaluate channels.

STEP 04Recommend & qualify

Quantify impact, state limitations and propose measurable experiments.

Population distinction

Q1 uses all users with positive revenue during the available full period. Q2–Q4 use mature users with a complete 90-day observation window. These populations should not be treated as identical.

Decision principle

Separate what the data demonstrates from what needs testing. For example, high channel revenue per user does not prove incremental ROI without spend and attribution data.

Q1

Who are the high-value customers?

Problem → approach → solution → evidence → business action

Problem statement

Define customer value in a way the business can act on.

A useful definition should distinguish customers who contribute disproportionately to revenue, rather than simply label every active user as valuable.

Approach
  1. Calculate user-level revenue using product-specific rules.
  2. Keep positive-revenue users for the relative ranking.
  3. Rank users by revenue and split them into five equal-sized groups.
  4. Define the top quintile as the high-value segment.

Q2

Which 20 new users should we watch each week?

Predict day-90 high value using only information available in the first 7 days.

Problem statement

Identify future high-value customers early enough to act.

The target is not to describe customers after 90 days. It is to rank new users using their first-week behaviour so teams can prioritise outreach.

Approach
FEATURE WINDOWDay 0–7

Signup to signup + 7 days, exclusive of the endpoint.

LABEL WINDOWDay 90

High-value status from mature 90-day revenue.

COHORT14,159

Mature users; 375 positives.

BASE RATE2.65%

Severe class imbalance.

Weekly watchlist operating steps
1 · COHORTCollect signups

Form weekly signup cohort.

2 · WAITComplete day 7

Do not score partial windows.

3 · SCORERank users

Apply model to eligible features.

4 · SELECTTop 20 users

Share list with growth/CRM team.

Only score after the full day-7 feature window is complete. Then sort by predicted score and take the top 20 for each weekly cohort.
Model quality, leakage and validation

Controls applied

  • Chronological split: train 9,911; validation 2,124; test 2,124.
  • Excluded target, identifiers, split labels and post-day-7 fields that could update later.
  • Used signup-time KYC and first-week behaviour/revenue features.
  • Commodities excluded from day-90 target due to execution timestamp limitation.

Limitations to disclose

  • Test metrics were inspected during model selection; not a fully untouched final estimate.
  • Class weighting may make raw probabilities poorly calibrated; ranking is the main purpose.
  • SHAP audit showed target label among predictor columns in an explanation output. Verify exact fitted feature matrix and rerun SHAP after removing target.
  • No verified row-level weekly top-20 IDs were available in the exported results, so no IDs are invented here.
Before production: rerun leakage-safe explainability, calibrate if probability thresholds will be used, and validate on a future untouched signup cohort.
Q3

How should marketing budget be allocated?

Compare acquisition quality and value — then identify what additional evidence is required to make a true ROI decision.

Problem statement

Which channels bring customers with stronger future value?

A high-volume channel is not necessarily the highest-quality channel. Compare high-value rate and revenue per acquired user, while checking sample sizes.

Approach
  1. Group users by acquisition channel.
  2. Count users and high-value users.
  3. Calculate high-value rate and revenue per user.
  4. Use the results to prioritise experiments, not claim profitability without spend data.

Business solution · where to focus
Test & scale selectively

Referral + Apple Search Ads

Strong observed high-value rates and revenue per user. Run capped, controlled experiments and validate incremental high-value acquisition and CAC.

Protect & optimise

Organic + Google Ads

Largest volumes and substantial total revenue. Improve onboarding and cohort quality; do not cut without incrementality evidence.

Audit

Ad partners + Snapchat

Review placement quality, fraud, retention and downstream economics before scaling spend.

Critical limitation: marketing spend, CAC, attribution quality and incremental lift were not provided. The data supports a customer-quality comparison, not a definitive ROI ranking or precise percentage budget split. The next experiment should track spend, CAC, incremental lift, payback and 90-day revenue.
Q4

What if a major revenue source declines?

Quantify exposure, stress-test the impact and define the next strategic growth bet.

Problem statement

Revenue concentration creates business risk.

If one product accounts for most revenue, a decline in that product can materially affect the business. We need to quantify the exposure and identify a credible adjacent source.

Approach
  1. Use mature day-90 users and observable revenue.
  2. Exclude commodities due to execution timestamp limitation.
  3. Calculate product revenue shares.
  4. Model 10%, 25% and 40% declines in F&O while holding other revenue constant.

Business solution · next bet

1. Protect the core

Strengthen reliability, support, risk communication and retention for existing F&O customers.

2. Grow the adjacent engine

Stocks already contribute 21.37% of day-90 revenue. Test relevant stock discovery, education and portfolio journeys.

3. Track diversification

Monitor non-F&O revenue per user, repeat activity, retention and cross-product adoption, with suitability guardrails.

Do not force migration into products that do not fit customer needs. The goal is a broader, resilient product mix built on genuine customer value.

07 · Final answer

What should Groww
do with these findings?

Convert the analysis into a measurable operating plan. The recommendations below are tied directly to the evidence and the limitations of the supplied data.

Priority 1

Build a weekly early-value engine

Score users after day 7, prioritise the top 20, and validate the model on a future untouched cohort.

Measure:

Precision@20 · lift@20 · revenue captured@20 · future-cohort performance

Priority 2

Protect the high-value cohort

Design retention, service and relevant engagement around the small segment driving most revenue.

Measure:

High-value retention · revenue per high-value user · support experience

Priority 3

Run acquisition incrementality tests

Test Referral and Apple Search Ads with clear control groups, spend tracking and CAC measurement.

Measure:

Incremental high-value users · CAC · payback · 90-day revenue

Priority 4

Diversify revenue responsibly

Protect F&O experience while building relevant stock-led journeys and monitoring non-F&O contribution.

Measure:

Non-F&O revenue share · product breadth · retention · suitability

Important assumptions & limitations
  • Q1 full-period and Q2–Q4 mature day-90 populations differ; comparisons should respect their cohort definitions.
  • Commodities are included in Q1 full-period revenue but excluded from day-90 outcomes due to unavailable reliable execution timestamp.
  • Channel metrics lack spend, CAC and incremental attribution, so they cannot establish channel profitability.
  • Model test metrics were inspected during selection; a future untouched cohort is required for unbiased final validation.
  • SHAP feature explanations require a leakage-safe rerun because the target appeared in a feature audit output.
  • Scenario analysis holds all non-F&O revenue constant and should be read as sensitivity analysis, not a forecast.
